Movement Sequencing

Perform dance steps in the correct order.

⏱ Duration

30 Minutes

📊 Difficulty

Advanced

🎯 Module

Performance Assessment

Final Performance Video

Learning Objectives

  • Define movement sequencing in dance.
  • Arrange basic movements in a logical order.
  • Perform a short dance sequence while maintaining rhythm and coordination.

Understanding Movement Sequencing

Movement sequencing is the process of organizing individual movements into a planned order to create a complete movement pattern or routine. In dance, this involves connecting steps, gestures, directions, and transitions so that movements flow from one action to the next (Krasnow & Wilmerding, 2015; National Core Arts Standards, 2014).

Krasnow and Wilmerding (2015) address the learning and control of movement patterns. The National Core Arts Standards describe dance learning through creating, performing, responding, and connecting, including organizing and refining artistic work for presentation.

Why It Matters

Movement sequencing helps dancers remember the order of steps, maintain continuity, and perform routines as a coordinated whole. It also allows students to combine previously learned skills—such as rhythm, coordination, balance, body control, footwork, and spatial awareness—into one performance.

Practice Activity: Create an Eight-Count Sequence

  1. Choose a basic step, such as step-touch.
  2. Add an arm movement.
  3. Include a change of direction.
  4. Add a brief balance pose.
  5. Arrange the movements into an eight-count pattern.
  6. Practise the sequence slowly, then repeat it with music.
  7. Work with a partner or group to refine the sequence and perform it together.

You can create your own movement combinations, provided the sequence is safe and appropriate for your skill level. Creating, practising, and refining dance work aligns with the creative and performance processes described in the National Core Arts Standards (2014).

Assessment

Create and perform an eight- to sixteen-count routine using at least three previously learned dance skills. Assess the order of movements, transitions, rhythm, coordination, spatial awareness, and overall control.

References

  • Krasnow, D., & Wilmerding, M. V. (2015). Motor Learning and Control for Dance: Principles and Practices for Performers and Teachers. Human Kinetics. https://us.humankinetics.com/products/motor-learning-and-control-for-dance
  • National Coalition for Core Arts Standards. (2014). National Core Arts Standards: Dance. https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/content/dance-introduction

💡 Remember

Practise your sequence in small sections and link them together before performing the whole routine.

Reflection

Which previously learned skills did you combine in your sequence, and how did they work together?
